About

I'm a PhD candidate in the Department of Mechanical Engineering at IIT Patna, working at the intersection of solid-state metal processing and computational modeling. My dissertation examines powder bed friction stir additive manufacturing (PBFS-AM) of AZ31B magnesium alloy — a technique that builds parts without melting the feedstock, avoiding the porosity and residual stress problems common to fusion-based additive processes.

My research spans microstructural characterization (EBSD, TEM, XCT, XRD), mechanical and corrosion testing, residual stress analysis, and computational modeling using physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) and ABAQUS finite element simulation.

Advised by Dr. Probir Saha, IIT Patna

Research

Powder Bed Friction Stir Additive Manufacturing of AZ31B

A solid-state additive process that consolidates magnesium powder feedstock through friction and severe plastic deformation rather than melting — targeting dense, low-porosity, low-residual-stress builds suitable for lightweight structural and biomedical applications.

Solid-state processing AZ31B Mg alloy DDRX microstructure EBSD / TEM / XCT / XRD

Tensile strengthup to 245 MPa
Residual stress21–29 MPa

Layer-Wise State-Evolution PINN Framework

A physics-informed neural network framework that models process-state evolution layer by layer through a PBFS-AM build, coupling governing thermomechanical physics with data-driven learning to predict microstructural and mechanical outcomes without full-scale finite element simulation at every layer.
